solid.Shape: S = hole (S, P, D, DEPTH)

solid.Shape: S = hole (…, Name, Value, …)

Drill holes into a shape.

S = hole (S, P, D, DEPTH) drills a hole of diameter D millimetres at every row of the M-by-3 matrix P, each starting at its point and running down the z axis, DEPTH millimetres deep. An Inf DEPTH drills right through, however thick the part. The point is where the hole’s axis meets the surface of the part, and DEPTH is measured from it, as a drawing dimensions a hole. The drill comes from outside: everything on the axis before the point is cleared too, at the widest diameter of the hole, so a hole entering a sloping or curved face is clean all round. A hole started under material that its axis passes through first cuts that material as well. All the holes are cut in one operation. A hole that would remove nothing, its axis missing the part or its point too far from it for DEPTH to reach, is an error naming the point, as a thread whose axis meets no bore or cylinder is for solid.Shape.thread.

P may instead be a geom.UCS, such as geom.UCS (V) picks off a face in the viewer: the hole starts at its origin and runs against its normal, which points out of the part, so its normal sets the direction. With 'At', a hole is drilled at every point given in the plane of the UCS instead, which places a bolt circle or a row of holes on a face in one call.

Tapping holes

D may instead name an ISO metric thread from M1 to M64 with a diameter and pitch of ISO 261: a coarse thread by its diameter, as 'M6', a fine one with its pitch, as 'M10x1.25'. The hole is then drilled for tapping that thread by hand, and the thread itself is cut by solid.Shape.thread. The drill is the one ISO 2306:1972 lists for the thread in its tables 1 and 2, so 'M6' drills 5 millimetres and 'M8' 6.8; for a thread it does not list, M60 and M64 among them, it is the rule ISO 2306 states, the nominal diameter less the pitch.

With 'Custom', D may name a thread of any diameter and pitch, as 'M16x0.8', or 'M6.35x1.27' for the 1/4-20 UNC of an imperial screw, whose basic profile is the same. Its drill is the nominal diameter less the pitch rounded to a step of 0.05 millimetres, the step nearest that inside the band below.

Every drill lies in the 6H tolerance band of the thread’s minor diameter, from D_1, the nominal diameter less 1.0825 P, up by the tolerance of ISO 965-1:1998, 13.3.2, table 3, so a drawing that states 6H is met. At a pitch of 0.25 and 0.2, where ISO 965-1 has no grade 6, the band is the coarsest grade it has, 5 and 4. Tough materials take a larger drill than soft ones, and 'Material' and 'Engagement' choose another drill inside the band. For a custom thread the drills are the steps of 0.05 millimetres, and a band too narrow to hold one, at the finest pitches, is an error naming the thread. A drill chosen otherwise is the ISO 2306 drill or one of the sizes of the twist drills in RUKO’s catalogue, chapter 1.01 Twist drills (2014): DIN 338 type N, HSS ground, article 214, 0.3 to 20 millimetres, pages 54 to 56; DIN 345 type N, HSS, article 204, 10 to 60 millimetres, pages 78 and 79.

A tapping hole is countersunk at its entry, as IS 1369 (Part 3):1995, prepared from DIN 76-1:1983, draws the entry of an internal thread: 120 degrees, and 1.05 times the nominal diameter across at the surface, the largest its band from the nominal diameter allows, 6.3 for M6, so that the tap starts square and the thread starts clean. A hole drilled through, with an Inf DEPTH or one that reaches past the part, is countersunk alike where its axis last leaves the part. 'Countersink' false leaves both out, and numbers size both. A tapping hole takes no counterbore.

A tapping hole is expected to be drilled, not milled, so a blind one ends in the cone the point of a 118 degree twist drill leaves, beyond DEPTH, as the drawing of a blind tapped hole in IS 1369 (Part 3) shows it. 'Tip' gives another angle, and false a flat bottom.

Name/Value pairs shape the hole:

'Direction'
The nonzero 3-element direction the holes run in from their points, [0, 0, -1] by default. It cannot be given when P is a geom.UCS.
'At'
An N-by-2 matrix of points in the plane of the geom.UCS P, one hole at each, all against its normal; the UCS origin alone by default. It applies only when P is a geom.UCS.
'Material'
The material tapped, for a named thread. A tough one, 'stainless' or 'nickel', takes the largest drill inside the band, and 'steel', 'aluminium', 'brass', 'castiron' and 'plastic' keep the ISO 2306 drill. The tough ones are those OSG’s chart Tap Drill Size and Pitch Diameter Limit, volume 3 (2024), names hard or tough: stainless steel, nickel steel, Monel, cast steel and drop forgings.
'Engagement'
The thread engagement for a named thread, as a percentage of the full thread in ISO’s terms, 100 drilling at D_1: the diameter asked is the nominal diameter less 1.0825 P times the percentage over 100, and the drill is the one nearest it inside the band. A percentage that falls outside the band is an error naming the thread and the band, from 78.2 to 100 for M6. It cannot be given with 'Material'.
'Custom'
True to take a named thread of any diameter and pitch, false by default, so that a name outside ISO 261, as a mistyped pitch, is an error. A thread of ISO 261 is drilled as without it.
'Clearance'
For a named thread, 'fine', 'medium' or 'coarse': the hole is the clearance hole of ISO 273 in that series for a screw of the thread, not a hole for tapping it, 6.6 for M6 in the medium series.
'Counterbore'
[CD, CDEPTH]: a flat-bottomed bore of diameter CD, wider than the hole, CDEPTH deep, shallower than the hole, for the head of a cap screw. With a named thread and 'Clearance', true takes the counterbore of IS 3406 (Part 2) for a socket head cap screw of ISO 4762, and 'washer' the one for the same screw on a plain washer. A tapping hole takes none.
'Countersink'
CD, [CD, ANGLE] or [CD, ANGLE, CDEPTH]: a conical seat of diameter CD at the surface, wider than the hole, with an included angle of ANGLE degrees, 90 by default as for ISO countersunk screws, below a cylinder of the same diameter CDEPTH deep, none by default. It must be shallower than the hole. With a named thread and 'Clearance', true takes the countersink of IS 3406 (Part 1) for the countersunk heads of ISO 7721, and 'socket' the one for a hexagon socket countersunk screw of ISO 10642. A tapping hole takes the countersink of an internal thread by default, as the tapping holes above describe; false leaves it out, and numbers replace it.
'Tip'
The included angle in degrees of the cone a twist drill leaves at the bottom of a blind hole, 118 for a standard drill, or false for none. It is beyond DEPTH, which is measured to the full diameter as a drawing dimensions it. A blind tapping hole is drilled, so it takes the point of a 118 degree drill by default; false leaves it flat-bottomed, as an end mill or a flat-bottom drill leaves it. Every other blind hole is flat-bottomed by default, as an end mill leaves it.

A counterbore and a countersink cannot both be given, and a tip applies only to a blind hole. The standard ones need a named thread, and those for a screw head 'Clearance', fine or medium; solid.holespec gives their sizes, and the sizes of every hole for a named thread, before it is drilled. A hole may break into another, but holes that would cut a solid of S into pieces are an error, as a bore reaching the roots of a knurl, which leaves its teeth loose. Drilling the empty shape leaves it empty.

 
 ## A plate with four counterbored holes for M6 cap screws
 S = solid.box (80, 40, 12);
 P = [10, 10, 12; 70, 10, 12; 10, 30, 12; 70, 30, 12];
 S = hole (S, P, 6.6, Inf, 'Counterbore', [11, 6.5]);
 
 ## Four holes tapped M5 in stainless steel on a bolt circle of 30
 S = solid.cylinder (25, 10);
 U = geom.UCS ([0, 0, 1], [0, 0, 10]);
 XY = 15 * [1, 0; 0, 1; -1, 0; 0, -1];
 S = hole (S, U, 'M5', 8, 'At', XY, 'Material', 'stainless');

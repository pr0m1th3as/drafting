Class Definition: solid.Shape

drafting: solid.Shape

A solid in millimetres, modelled through Open CASCADE.

solid.Shape holds the exact boundary of one or more solids: every face a plane, cylinder, cone, sphere, torus or spline surface, every edge a line, circle or curve, with nothing approximated by facets. It is what the functions of the solid namespace create, what the boolean operators combine, and what its write method saves as STEP for exchange or STL for printing.

Shapes are made by solid.box, solid.cylinder, solid.cone, solid.sphere and solid.torus, from regions by solid.extrude, solid.revolve, solid.loft, solid.sweep and solid.helix, and from files by solid.read, and combined by three methods, each taking any number of shapes:

MethodResult
unionthe material of any of them
subtractthe material of the first that is in none of the others
intersectthe material common to all of them

hull wraps shapes and points in their convex hull.

 
 plate = solid.box (80, 40, 12);
 bore = translate (solid.cylinder (4, 12), [20, 20, 0]);
 part = subtract (plate, bore);
 volume (part)
 ⇒ 3.7797e+04

Features are worked on a shape by methods: hole drills plain, counterbored, countersunk and tapping holes, pocket cuts a region into it, fillet and chamfer round or bevel edges, and shell hollows the shape. The edges and faces they act on are chosen by edges and faces, by the kind of curve or surface, by direction and by position. section cuts a shape with a plane into regions, which the solid functions build from again, and projection gives the outline of a shape seen along a direction as regions in the same way.

The class is a value class. Every operation returns a new shape and leaves its operands unchanged, and a copy is independent of the original. The empty shape adds nothing to a union and empties an intersection, and a boolean whose result holds no material, such as the intersection of two solids that do not meet, returns it. A shape is saved and loaded with save and load like any other value.

The solid namespace needs Open CASCADE, which the package uses where it is found when the package is built. On a build without it, the empty shape can still be made, but every function and method that needs the library raises an error saying so.

See also: solid.box, solid.read, solid.Shape.write, solid.Shape.show

Source Code: solid.Shape

The solid.Shape class contains the following properties:

An N-by-3 matrix of red, green and blue from 0 to 1, a row for each of the shape’s N solids in the order solid.Shape.numsolids counts them, a row of NaN for a solid without a colour, or empty when none has one. Assigning a single row colours every solid. The viewer shows it, and a STEP file carries it out and in. A transform keeps it, and each solid a boolean or a feature makes takes the colour of the first coloured solid it came from, so parts apart keep their own; the tools of subtract give none.

The solid.Shape class offers the following public methods:

solid.Shape: S = solid.Shape ()

solid.Shape: S = solid.Shape (DATA)

S = solid.Shape () returns the empty shape, which holds nothing.

S = solid.Shape (DATA) wraps DATA, the bytes of a shape in Open CASCADE’s binary format. This is how the functions of the solid namespace return what Open CASCADE computed, and it is not for direct use: bytes that do not begin with that format’s signature are refused.

solid.Shape: C = union (A, B, …)

C = union (A, B, …) takes any number of solid.Shape objects and combines them in one operation, which is several times faster than combining them a pair at a time. Faces that end up in one plane and edges that end up on one line are merged, so the union of two blocks that share a face reads as one block of six faces. Empty shapes add nothing, and the union of a single shape is that shape.

See also: solid.Shape.subtract, solid.Shape.intersect

solid.Shape: C = subtract (A, B, …)

C = subtract (A, B, …) removes from A the material of every later shape, all in one operation. This is how a hole, a pocket or a slot is cut: model the material to remove and subtract it. Drilling a plate with a hundred holes in one call is an order of magnitude faster than a hundred calls of one hole each.

Removing empty shapes, or nothing, leaves A, and removing anything from the empty shape leaves the empty shape.

See also: solid.Shape.union, solid.Shape.intersect

solid.Shape: C = intersect (A, B, …)

C = intersect (A, B, …) keeps only what lies inside every shape given. Shapes that do not all meet, and an empty shape among them, give the empty shape. The intersection of a single shape is that shape.

See also: solid.Shape.union, solid.Shape.subtract

solid.Shape: M = tessellate (S)

solid.Shape: M = tessellate (S, TOL)

M = tessellate (S, TOL) returns a polymesh.Mesh of the surface of S: flat triangles whose corners lie on it and which stray from it by at most TOL millimetres, 0.01 by default, nor span more than 20 degrees of a curved face. The triangles meet edge to edge, sharing their corners, and are turned outwards, so the mesh of a solid is closed. A coloured shape gives each triangle the colour of its solid as the mesh’s FaceColour, and the grey [0.72, 0.74, 0.78] where its solid has none. The empty shape gives the empty mesh.

This is the mesh write writes to STL, OBJ, PLY and 3MF, and it can be shown, cut, measured, coloured and written as any other.

 
 M = tessellate (solid.cylinder (4, 12), 0.05);
 write (M, 'pin.obj');

See also: polymesh.Mesh, solid.Shape.write, solid.polyhedron

solid.Shape: show (S)

solid.Shape: V = show (S)

show (S) shows the shape S in a model.Viewer, a window drawn by Open CASCADE in a process of its own. Every variable gets a viewer of its own, titled with its name: the first show (part) opens the window for part, and every later one redraws that window in place and keeps the camera where it was, while show (tool) uses another. So a script that ends by showing its parts can be run again and again, clear all and all, and each part changes in its own window. A shape given as an expression rather than a variable, such as show (fillet (part, E, 3)), has no name, and all such shapes share one window.

Drag with the left mouse button to rotate, the middle one to pan, and turn the wheel to zoom; F fits the part to the window and 0, 1, 2 and 3 turn it to the isometric, front, top and right views. Closing a window ends its viewer, and the next show of that variable opens a new one. Showing the empty shape opens a variable’s window before there is anything to draw in it.

V = show (S) also returns the viewer. Assigning to V.Shape redraws it at once, and model.Viewer.pick picks edges and faces with the mouse:

 
 part = solid.box (80, 40, 12);
 V = show (part);
 E = pick (V, 'edge');     # click the edges, then press Enter
 V.Shape = fillet (V.Shape, E, 3);

Calling show (S) is the same as assigning S to the Shape of the variable’s viewer. Nothing else redraws it: changing the variable that was shown does not, until it is shown again.

The viewer is built with the package when Open CASCADE and X11 are found, and needs a display to run. It runs on Linux.

See also: model.Viewer, model.Viewer.pick, polymesh.Mesh.show

solid.Shape: write (S, FILE)

solid.Shape: write (S, FILE, 'Tolerance', TOL)

write (S, FILE) writes the shape S in the format named by the extension of FILE, in either case:

.step
STEP (ISO 10303-21, application protocol 214), in millimetres. The exact geometry is kept, so this is the file to send to a CAD program or to another manufacturer. The part is named after the base name of FILE, and each solid carries its solid.Shape.Colour.
.stl, .obj, .ply, .3mf
A mesh of triangles in millimetres, the mesh solid.Shape.tessellate makes, written by polymesh.Mesh.write: binary STL, OBJ, binary PLY, or 3MF, the archive slicers take. The facets approximate every curved surface; their vertices lie on it. OBJ, PLY and 3MF carry the solids’ colours on their triangles; STL has none.

write (…, 'Tolerance', TOL) sets, for a mesh, the largest distance in millimetres between a facet and the true surface. It is 0.01 by default, well below what a printer resolves; a larger value gives a smaller file. No facet spans more than 20 degrees of a curved surface, whatever TOL. A STEP file is exact and takes no tolerance.

See also: solid.read, solid.Shape.tessellate, polymesh.Mesh.write

solid.Shape: S = translate (S, V)

V is a 3-element vector in millimetres.

See also: solid.Shape.rotate, solid.Shape.mirror, solid.Shape.scale

solid.Shape: S = rotate (S, ANGLE, AXIS)

solid.Shape: S = rotate (S, ANGLE, AXIS, P)

ANGLE is in degrees and turns the shape anticlockwise when seen from the tip of AXIS looking back, the right-hand rule. AXIS is a nonzero 3-element direction, and P a point on the axis, the origin by default.

See also: solid.Shape.translate, solid.Shape.mirror, solid.Shape.scale

solid.Shape: S = mirror (S, N)

solid.Shape: S = mirror (S, N, P)

The plane passes through the point P, the origin by default, with the nonzero 3-element normal N. mirror (S, [1, 0, 0]) reflects in the yz plane. A reflection turns a right-handed part into its left-handed twin; the result is a valid solid of the same volume.

See also: solid.Shape.translate, solid.Shape.rotate, solid.Shape.scale

solid.Shape: S = scale (S, F)

solid.Shape: S = scale (S, F, P)

Every length is multiplied by the positive factor F, so the volume grows by its cube. P, the origin by default, is the point that stays where it is.

See also: solid.Shape.translate, solid.Shape.rotate, solid.Shape.mirror

solid.Shape: V = volume (S)

The volume is computed from the exact surfaces, not from facets, to a relative error of about 1e-9. The empty shape has volume zero.

See also: solid.Shape.area, solid.Shape.centroid

solid.Shape: A = area (S)

The area is the sum over every face, computed from the exact surfaces. A flat face is measured to a relative error of about 1e-9, whatever its edges. A curved face is measured as Open CASCADE integrates it: exactly for a cylinder, a cone, a sphere or a torus, to about 1e-4 for a face swept from a spline. The empty shape has area zero.

See also: solid.Shape.volume

solid.Shape: C = centroid (S)

For a part of uniform density this is its centre of mass, computed as the volume is. The empty shape has no centroid and returns an empty C, and a shape that encloses no volume returns NaN in every coordinate.

See also: solid.Shape.volume, solid.Shape.bbox

solid.Shape: B = bbox (S)

solid.Shape: [B, L] = bbox (S)

B is the 1-by-6 vector [xmin, ymin, zmin, xmax, ymax, zmax] in millimetres, and L the 1-by-3 vector of the lengths along each axis.

The box is computed from the exact surfaces, with no allowance for the tolerance Open CASCADE carries on its edges, so a cylinder of radius 4 reaches exactly 4 from its axis. On planes, cylinders, cones and spheres it is tight; on a torus or a spline surface it may exceed the true extent by up to 1e-7 millimetres, the precision Open CASCADE computes it to there. The empty shape has no extent and returns an empty B and L.

See also: solid.Shape.centroid, geom.bbox

solid.Shape: TF = isvalid (S)

The check confirms that the faces close up, that the edges lie on the surfaces they bound, and that nothing intersects itself. Every shape made by the solid namespace should pass; a failure means that an operation went wrong, usually a boolean between faces that nearly but not quite coincide, and the shape should not be written or drawn. The empty shape is valid.

solid.Shape: TF = isempty (S)

solid.Shape: N = numsolids (S)

A boolean can leave more than one: cutting a bar across its whole section leaves two.

See also: solid.Shape.numfaces, solid.Shape.numedges

solid.Shape: N = numfaces (S)

A box has six. A cylinder has three, its curved side being one face.

See also: solid.Shape.numsolids, solid.Shape.numedges

solid.Shape: N = numedges (S)

An edge shared by two faces is one edge. A box has twelve. A cylinder has three: its two circles and the seam where its curved face closes on itself, which bounds that face but is not a feature of the part.

See also: solid.Shape.numsolids, solid.Shape.numfaces

solid.Shape: E = edges (S)

solid.Shape: E = edges (S, Name, Value, …)

E = edges (S) returns the indices of the edges of S as a row vector, in the order solid.Shape.numedges counts them. Seam edges, where a curved face closes on itself, and the degenerate edges at the poles of a sphere are left out: they bound faces without being features of the part, and nothing can be done to them.

The indices belong to S alone. Every operation that makes a new shape numbers its edges afresh, so choose edges from the shape the feature is applied to, immediately before applying it.

Name/Value pairs narrow the choice, and an edge is returned only when it meets all of them:

'Type'
The kind of curve the edge lies on: 'line', 'circle', 'ellipse', 'bspline' or 'other', or a cell array of several.
'Direction'
A nonzero 3-element vector. A straight edge is chosen when it runs parallel to it, either way; a circle or an ellipse when its axis does. Other edges have no direction and are never chosen.
'Within'
A box [xmin, ymin, zmin, xmax, ymax, zmax], as solid.Shape.bbox returns one. An edge is chosen when it lies wholly inside, to within 1e-6 millimetres.
'Face'
Face indices, as solid.Shape.faces returns them. An edge is chosen when it bounds any of these faces, so all the edges round a face can be rounded or chamfered in one step: fillet (S, edges (S, 'Face', F), R).
 
 B = solid.box (10, 20, 30);
 E = edges (B, 'Direction', [0, 0, 1]);    # the four upright edges
 B = fillet (B, E, 2);

The empty shape has no edges.

See also: solid.Shape.faces, solid.Shape.fillet, solid.Shape.chamfer

solid.Shape: F = faces (S)

solid.Shape: F = faces (S, Name, Value, …)

F = faces (S) returns the indices of the faces of S as a row vector, in the order solid.Shape.numfaces counts them. The indices belong to S alone: every operation that makes a new shape numbers its faces afresh, so choose faces from the shape the feature is applied to, immediately before applying it.

Name/Value pairs narrow the choice, and a face is returned only when it meets all of them:

'Type'
The kind of surface the face lies on: 'plane', 'cylinder', 'cone', 'sphere', 'torus', 'revolution', 'extrusion', 'bspline' or 'other', or a cell array of several.
'Normal'
A nonzero 3-element vector. A flat face is chosen when its outward normal points the same way, so [0, 0, 1] picks faces looking up and not those looking down. Curved faces are never chosen.
'Axis'
A nonzero 3-element vector. A face on a cylinder, cone, torus or other surface of revolution is chosen when its axis runs parallel to it, either way.
'Within'
A box [xmin, ymin, zmin, xmax, ymax, zmax], as solid.Shape.bbox returns one. A face is chosen when it lies wholly inside, to within 1e-6 millimetres.

The empty shape has no faces.

See also: solid.Shape.edges, solid.Shape.shell

solid.Shape: S = fillet (S, E, R)

S = fillet (S, E, R) rounds the edges of S indexed by E, as solid.Shape.edges returns them, to the radius R millimetres. An outside edge loses material and an inside edge gains it, as a fillet on a casting or a radius left by a cutter does. Where rounded edges meet at a corner the corner is blended.

An edge is rounded together with every edge that runs on from it smoothly, tangent where they meet, as Open CASCADE rounds a chain. On a box whose upright edges are already rounded, choosing one top edge rounds the whole top rim.

A radius too large for the faces beside an edge cannot be built and raises an error, as does an edge that cannot be rounded at all.

See also: solid.Shape.edges, solid.Shape.chamfer

solid.Shape: S = chamfer (S, E, D)

solid.Shape: S = chamfer (S, E, [D1, D2], F)

solid.Shape: S = chamfer (S, E, D, F, 'Angle', A)

S = chamfer (S, E, D) cuts the edges of S indexed by E, as solid.Shape.edges returns them, back by D millimetres on both faces, so a chamfer on a square corner is the 45 degree bevel a drawing calls D x 45. An edge is bevelled together with every edge that runs on from it smoothly, tangent where they meet, as Open CASCADE bevels a chain. On a box whose upright edges are already rounded, choosing one top edge bevels the whole top rim.

S = chamfer (S, E, [D1, D2], F) cuts each edge back by D1 along the face F, a face index as solid.Shape.faces returns one, and by D2 along the other face of the edge. Every edge must bound F, as the edges round a face do: edges (S, 'Face', F).

S = chamfer (S, E, D, F, 'Angle', A) cuts each edge back by D along the face F, with the chamfer face meeting F at A degrees, in the range (0, 90): the chamfer a drawing calls D x A. Along the other face it reaches D * tand (A), so 45 degrees is the equal chamfer.

A chamfer too large for the faces beside an edge cannot be built and raises an error, as does an edge that cannot be chamfered at all.

See also: solid.Shape.edges, solid.Shape.fillet

solid.Shape: S = shell (S, F, T)

solid.Shape: S = shell (…, 'Outward', TF)

solid.Shape: S = shell (…, 'Thickness', {FACES, T2, …})

S = shell (S, F, T) removes the faces of S indexed by F, as solid.Shape.faces returns them, and hollows what is left to walls T millimetres thick, as a box, a cup or an enclosure is made. The walls grow inwards, so the outside of the part keeps its size and the inner corners stay sharp. With F empty no face is removed and the result is a closed shell around a sealed cavity.

S = shell (…, 'Outward', true) grows the walls outwards instead, so the inside keeps the size and shape of S and the outside grows by T: the way to make a box that a part of the shape of S fits into. It is false by default.

S = shell (…, 'Thickness', {FACES, T2, …}) gives the faces indexed by FACES walls T2 thick instead of T, and so on for every pair in the cell array: a thicker floor, a thinner lid. The faces come from solid.Shape.faces, none may be opened, and none may be given two thicknesses:

 
 ## A box of 2 walls with a floor 4 thick, open at the top
 B = solid.box (60, 40, 30);
 B = shell (B, faces (B, 'Normal', [0, 0, 1]), 2, ...
            'Thickness', {faces(B, 'Normal', [0, 0, -1]), 4});

Walls too thick for the shape, which would meet in the middle, cannot be built and raise an error.

 
 ## An open box of 1 mm walls
 B = solid.box (10, 20, 30);
 B = shell (B, faces (B, 'Normal', [0, 0, 1]), 1);
 volume (B)
 ⇒ 1824.0

See also: solid.Shape.faces

solid.Shape: S = hole (S, P, D, DEPTH)

solid.Shape: S = hole (…, Name, Value, …)

S = hole (S, P, D, DEPTH) drills a hole of diameter D millimetres at every row of the M-by-3 matrix P, each starting at its point and running down the z axis, DEPTH millimetres deep. An Inf DEPTH drills right through, however thick the part. The point is where the hole’s axis meets the surface of the part, and DEPTH is measured from it, as a drawing dimensions a hole. The drill comes from outside: everything on the axis before the point is cleared too, at the widest diameter of the hole, so a hole entering a sloping or curved face is clean all round. A hole started under material that its axis passes through first cuts that material as well. All the holes are cut in one operation.

D may instead name an ISO metric coarse thread, such as 'M6' or 'M2.5', from M1 to M64: the hole is then drilled at the tapping size for that thread, its nominal diameter less its pitch, so 'M6' drills 5 millimetres. The thread itself is not modelled.

Name/Value pairs shape the hole:

'Direction'
The nonzero 3-element direction the holes run in from their points, [0, 0, -1] by default.
'Counterbore'
[CD, CDEPTH]: a flat-bottomed bore of diameter CD, wider than the hole, CDEPTH deep, shallower than the hole, for the head of a cap screw.
'Countersink'
CD or [CD, ANGLE]: a conical seat of diameter CD at the surface, wider than the hole, with an included angle of ANGLE degrees, 90 by default as for ISO countersunk screws. It must be shallower than the hole.
'Tip'
The included angle in degrees of the cone a twist drill leaves at the bottom of a blind hole, 118 for a standard drill. It is beyond DEPTH, which is measured to the full diameter as a drawing dimensions it. By default a blind hole is flat-bottomed.

A counterbore and a countersink cannot both be given, and a tip applies only to a blind hole. Drilling the empty shape leaves it empty.

 
 ## A plate with four counterbored holes for M6 cap screws
 S = solid.box (80, 40, 12);
 P = [10, 10, 12; 70, 10, 12; 10, 30, 12; 70, 30, 12];
 S = hole (S, P, 6.6, Inf, 'Counterbore', [11, 6.5]);

See also: solid.Shape.subtract, solid.cylinder

solid.Shape: S = pocket (S, R, DEPTH)

solid.Shape: S = pocket (…, 'Taper', A)

S = pocket (S, R, DEPTH) cuts the geom.Region R into S, DEPTH millimetres deep from the region’s plane, against the normal of its geom.UCS: a recess, a slot, a cavity. Lay the region on a face with the face’s UCS, whose normal points out of the part, as geom.UCS (V) picks one. An Inf DEPTH cuts right through.

The holes of the region are left standing as islands, so a pocket round a boss is one region with the boss as its hole, and fillet on the region rounds the pocket’s corners as a milling cutter leaves them.

The cutter comes from outside, as for solid.Shape.hole: everything inside the outline beyond the region’s plane, on the side its normal points to, is cleared too, so a pocket drawn on a sloping or uneven face is clean all round. A pocket drawn under material that its outline passes through first cuts that material as well.

S = pocket (…, 'Taper', A) leans the pocket’s walls in by A degrees as they go deeper, the draft of a moulded recess, as solid.extrude tapers a wall; the islands widen to match. A is in the range (-90, 90). A region with splines takes no taper, as for solid.extrude.

Pocketing the empty shape leaves it empty.

 
 ## A slot 30 by 8 with round ends, 4 deep in the top of a plate
 S = solid.box (80, 40, 12);
 R = geom.Region ([25, 16, 0; 55, 16, 1; 55, 24, 0; 25, 24, 1]);
 R.UCS = geom.UCS ([0, 0, 1], [0, 0, 12]);
 S = pocket (S, R, 4);

See also: solid.Shape.hole, solid.extrude, geom.Region.fillet

solid.Shape: R = section (S, U)

R = section (S, U) cuts the shape S with the plane of the geom.UCS U and returns the cut as a 1-by-N cell array of geom.Region objects in U, one for each separate piece of it, largest first. A piece is an outline with the holes in it; a piece standing in a hole of another is a piece of its own. A face of S lying in the plane is part of the cut, so a box cut in the plane of its base gives its base. Where the plane only touches S, along an edge or at a point, or misses it, the cut has no area and R is the empty cell (1, 0).

The cut is exact enough to build from again. Straight edges are straight segments and circles are arcs, a whole circle two half arcs. An ellipse or another conic, as a plane cuts from a cylinder or a cone at a slant, is a rational geom.Spline that follows it exactly; any other curve, as from a torus or a spline surface, is the B-spline Open CASCADE computes for it.

 
 ## A plate with a bore, cut through the middle and made again
 S = solid.extrude (geom.Region ([0, 0; 60, 0; 60, 40; 0, 40], ...
                                 {[20, 20, 1; 40, 20, 1]}), 8);
 R = section (S, geom.UCS ([0, 0, 1], [0, 0, 4]));
 T = solid.extrude (R{1}, 2);

See also: geom.Region, geom.UCS, solid.extrude

solid.Shape: R = projection (S)

solid.Shape: R = projection (S, U)

R = projection (S, U) projects the shape S along the normal of the geom.UCS U onto its plane and returns the area it covers as a 1-by-N cell array of geom.Region objects in U, one for each separate piece of it, largest first. This is the shadow the shape casts on the plane in light falling along the normal: a bore seen along its axis is a hole in the outline, and seen from the side it is not there. A piece standing in a hole of another is a piece of its own. Where the plane lies along its normal changes only the plane the regions lie in. The empty shape has no outline, and R is the empty cell (1, 0).

R = projection (S) projects onto the xy plane, as seen from above, which is what OpenSCAD’s projection does.

The outline is drawn from the edges of S and the outlines of its curved faces. Straight edges are straight segments and a circle seen square on is a circle; a circle seen at a slant is the ellipse it makes, and the outline of a cylinder, a cone or a sphere is exact too. That of a torus or of a spline surface is the B-spline Open CASCADE fits to it. section cuts a shape with a plane instead.

 
 ## A plate with a bore, seen from above and from the front
 S = subtract (solid.box (80, 40, 12), ...
               translate (solid.cylinder (4, 12), [20, 20, 0]));
 top = projection (S);
 numel (top{1}.Holes)
 ⇒ 1
 front = projection (S, geom.UCS ([0, -1, 0], [0, 0, 0]));
 numel (front{1}.Holes)
 ⇒ 0

See also: solid.Shape.section, geom.Region, geom.UCS

solid.Shape: H = hull (A, B, …)

solid.Shape: H = hull (…, 'Tolerance', TOL)

H = hull (A, B, …) returns the smallest convex solid that holds every shape and point given, the shape a sheet shrunk round them takes. Each argument is a solid.Shape or an N-by-3 matrix of points. This is OpenSCAD’s hull of solids: a slot from two cylinders, a rounded block from eight spheres at its corners, a tapered arm from the bosses at its ends. Empty shapes add nothing, and the hull of nothing is the empty shape.

The hull has flat faces. The surface of each shape is taken as the points of a triangulation that strays no further than TOL from it, 0.01 by default, so the hull lies inside the exact one by at most TOL; where the exact hull is flat, as between boxes, it is exact and its triangles meet as whole faces. Open CASCADE has no exact hull of curved faces.

 
 ## A slot 30 long and 10 wide, 4 deep, cut through a plate
 C = solid.cylinder (5, 4);
 slot = hull (C, translate (C, [20, 0, 0]));
 plate = subtract (translate (solid.box (50, 30, 4), [-15, -15, 0]), ...
                   slot);

See also: geom.Region.hull, solid.polyhedron, solid.Shape.union

solid.Shape: S = resize (S, SZ)

solid.Shape: S = resize (S, SZ, 'Uniform', TF)

S = resize (S, SZ) scales the shape evenly so that its bounding box is as large as it can be within the sizes SZ = [x, y, z] in millimetres; a size of 0 leaves that direction free. resize (S, [30, 0, 0]) makes the shape 30 long in x and keeps its proportions. The corner of the box at the least x, y and z stays where it is, and every face keeps its type: a cylinder stays a cylinder.

With 'Uniform' set to false each direction given is scaled to its size on its own and a direction left at 0 keeps its size, as OpenSCAD’s resize does. Flat faces stay planes, but a curved face stretched so is no longer a cylinder, a cone or a sphere: Open CASCADE carries it as the exact B-spline surface it becomes, so solid.Shape.faces finds a stretched cylinder among the faces of type 'bspline', not 'cylinder'.

 
 ## A sphere of diameter 10 stretched into an ellipsoid 40 by 20 by 10
 E = resize (solid.sphere (5), [40, 20, 0], 'Uniform', false);

See also: solid.Shape.scale, solid.ellipsoid, geom.Region.resize

solid.Shape: C = copy (S, D)

C = copy (S, D) places a copy of the shape moved by each row of D, an N-by-3 matrix of offsets in millimetres, and returns them as one shape, united as solid.Shape.union unites shapes. The shape itself is among them only where a row of D is zero. Copies apart from one another are kept side by side, separate solids of the one shape, with no boolean to compute; only copies that overlap or touch are fused, so a hundred pins standing apart cost little more than one. This is OpenSCAD’s for loop of translate.

See also: solid.Shape.rectarray, solid.Shape.polararray, geom.Region.copy

solid.Shape: C = rectarray (S, COUNT, SPACING)

C = rectarray (S, COUNT, SPACING) places COUNT = [nx, ny, nz] copies of the shape along the x, y and z axes, SPACING = [dx, dy, dz] millimetres apart, the first where the shape is; a negative spacing runs the other way. It returns them as one shape, as solid.Shape.copy does. This is a grid of holes cut into a plate in one subtraction, or of ribs, pins or bosses standing on it.

 
 ## A plate 70 by 50 with four rows of six holes of diameter 4
 H = rectarray (translate (solid.cylinder (2, 5), [10, 10, 0]), ...
                [6, 4, 1], [10, 10, 0]);
 P = subtract (solid.box (70, 50, 5), H);

See also: solid.Shape.copy, solid.Shape.polararray

solid.Shape: C = polararray (S, N, ANGLE)

solid.Shape: C = polararray (S, N, ANGLE, AXIS)

solid.Shape: C = polararray (S, N, ANGLE, AXIS, P)

solid.Shape: C = polararray (…, 'Rotate', TF)

C = polararray (S, N, ANGLE, AXIS, P) places N copies of the shape round the axis through the point P in the direction AXIS, the z axis through the origin by default, the first where the shape is and the rest turned on by the right-hand rule, the other way for a negative ANGLE. A whole turn, ANGLE of 360, spaces them evenly ANGLE / N apart; a part of a turn puts one at each end, ANGLE / (N - 1) apart. It returns them as one shape, as solid.Shape.copy does.

Each copy is turned as it goes round, as the holes of a bolt circle, the teeth of a wheel or the blades of a fan are. With 'Rotate' set to false each copy keeps the shape’s own direction, moved as the centre of its bounding box moves.

 
 ## A flange of diameter 60 with six bolt holes of 6 on a circle of 40
 H = polararray (translate (solid.cylinder (3, 8), [20, 0, 0]), 6, 360);
 F = subtract (solid.cylinder (30, 8), solid.cylinder (10, 8), H);

See also: solid.Shape.copy, solid.Shape.rectarray, solid.Shape.rotate

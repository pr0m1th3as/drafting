Knurls

The standard behind solid.Shape.knurl: the types of knurl it makes, their profile and pitch, where each number comes from, and what the package decides where the standard leaves the choice open.

The source

DIN 82:1973 is the standard for knurls, and it is sold by DIN. It was read in IS 3403:1981, which the Bureau of Indian Standards prepared from it and publishes free, so any value here can be checked without buying the standard.

IS 3403 (First Revision):1981In its explanatory note: what the letters of a type stand for, the angle of 30 degrees fixed as the standard value, the normal profile angle, and the pitch of 2 dropped. The standard itself

The types

As the note of IS 3403 explains them, the first letter, R, marks a knurl, as against the knurling wheel that makes it. The second names the basic type: A for grooves along the axis, B for one set of grooves at an angle, G for two sets at opposite angles, K for a cross knurl of grooves along the axis and round it. The third gives the direction or the form: A along the axis, L left-hand, R right-hand, E points raised, V points indented.

A right-hand knurl runs as a right-hand thread does, so a positive 'Angle' is right-handed and RBL is RBR seen in a mirror. The standard draws the grooves of RBL, RBR, RGE and RGV at 30 degrees from the axis, and the note of IS 3403 fixes that angle as the standard value; it is the default of 'Angle'. Any other angle is taken, and so are two sets at different angles, the cross knurl being the two sets at 0 and 90 degrees.

The profile and the pitch

The profile angle, between the flanks of a tooth, is 90 degrees, and the note of IS 3403 calls that the normal value; 'Profile' takes any other. The pitch is measured square to the grooves, as the sections of the standard draw it, and the standard lists 0.5, 0.6, 0.8, 1.0, 1.2 and 1.6 millimetres. The note of IS 3403 says a pitch of 2 was dropped as scarcely used, and that no pitch is recommended for a given diameter, since earlier recommendations did not suit every use. knurl takes any positive pitch.

The count round the face

The standard does not say how the grooves meet round the face. A knurl that closes on itself must hold a whole number of grooves round it, so the package fits the pitch to the nearest that gives one: the count of a set at the angle A from the axis is π d cos A / P, rounded, d the diameter of the face, and the pitch is π d cos A over that count. Rings, at 90 degrees, are counted along the length of the face the same way. On a face 28 across, a pitch of 0.8 at 30 degrees gives 95 grooves each way and a pitch of 0.80195.

The first groove of every set starts at the lower end of the face, at the x direction of its cylinder. The standard sets no phase.

The depth

The standard gives no depth for the finished knurl. Its nominal diameter, the one a drawing states, is the outside diameter of the finished knurl, and that is the diameter of the face the package knurls. By default the grooves are cut to full depth, where the flanks of two grooves meet in a point: P / (2 tan (α / 2)), half the pitch at 90 degrees, with the pitch as fitted. Where two sets fit to different pitches, both are cut to the depth of the finer. A DEPTH less than that leaves flat lands at the nominal diameter between the grooves; a greater one is refused.

The blank to turn

Knurling presses material up out of the grooves, so the blank is turned smaller than the nominal diameter d1. The standard gives its initial diameter d2 for a profile of 90 degrees, and notes that the factors take no account of the grooves' rounding or of the material:

  • RAA, RBL and RBR: d2 = d1 − 0.5 P;
  • RGE and RKE: d2 = d1 − 0.67 P;
  • RGV and RKV: d2 = d1 − 0.33 P.

These are the factors of the package's full-depth teeth, whose volume is that of the blank. A set of V grooves takes half the material between the nominal diameter and the root; a raised point is a pyramid on its base, which holds a third of the block it stands in, so raised points take two thirds; an indented point is the same pyramid sunk into the block, and takes a third. Measured on a face 28 across and 10 long at a pitch of 0.8, as the diameter of a cylinder of the same length and volume:

RKE differs most because its rings are fitted along the face's length of 10, 13 of them at a pitch of 0.769, and both sets are cut to the depth of that finer pitch, so its grooves along the axis stop short of full depth. The package does not compute the blank; it is on the drawing for the machinist, from the formulas above.

The ends

The standard does not treat the ends of a knurl. The package takes a fillet or a 45 degree chamfer at a free end, given to knurl with 'Fillet' or 'Chamfer', and the knurl runs out over it as it does on a blank whose edge was rounded or chamfered before knurling. An end that meets a shoulder takes neither. Each end must meet a flat face square to the axis, so a fillet or chamfer cut on the blank before knurling is refused; it is given to knurl instead.

How the knurl is built

A knurl is not cut. It is laid out on the face unrolled, where every flank of a groove is a plane in the angle round the axis and the length along it, and each piece of the pattern is built as a face on its true surface: a cylinder at the nominal diameter or at the root, a spline surface for a flank, a torus for a fillet, a cone for a chamfer. The face of the part is replaced by the knurled skin, and each end face takes the knurl's outline in place of its circle, so no boolean is run. Over every type, with fillets and chamfers above and below the depth, the surface lies within 0.0000002 millimetres of the profile computed from its formula. A knob 28 across and 10 high knurled RGE at 0.8 takes about two seconds. The same knob with a bore and a hexagon pocket, knurled at a pitch of 2.07 and 45 degrees, takes under one, against 23 seconds when its grooves were cut with booleans.

What is not done

  • The rounding of the grooves: the teeth are cut to the sharp profile, which the standard's factors also take.
  • The knurling wheels of IS 6776, and the choice of wheel for each type, which IS 3403 gives in its appendix.
  • The designation on a drawing, as Knurl RKV 08 IS : 3403 in the Indian form, which comes with the drawings of solids.
  • Knurls on a cone, on a face that is not whole round its axis, or inside a bore.

Editions

IS 3403 (First Revision):1981 was read, as printed with its reaffirmation of 2002. It was prepared from DIN 82 of 1973, whose edition of January 1973 DIN lists as current, checked in October 2026; it replaced DIN 82 of 1940.

Clearance holes, counterbores and countersinks

The standards behind solid.Shape.hole with 'Clearance' and its standard counterbores and countersinks, and behind solid.holespec, which returns their sizes before a hole is drilled: where each number comes from, and what the package takes from each standard and what it leaves.

The sources

Every number is read from a published standard, never from memory or from another library. ISO 273 was read in the Indian Standard that adopts it without change. The counterbores and countersinks were read in an Indian Standard of their own, IS 3406, which the Bureau of Indian Standards prepared from DIN 74 of 1980; both parts are published free, so any value here can be checked without buying a standard.

Clearance holes

A clearance hole is drilled for a screw to pass through, not to be tapped. ISO 273 gives it in three series by the nominal diameter of the thread: fine, for precise location, medium for general engineering, and coarse. Its table is the one 'Clearance' reads, so 'M6' with 'Clearance', 'medium' drills 6.6. The table is read by the diameter alone, so a fine thread takes the hole of its coarse one, and a custom thread the hole of its diameter.

ISO 273 lists 35 diameters from M1 to M64, fewer than ISO 261's 53. A diameter it does not list, such as M5.5, M9 or M11, has no clearance hole, and 'Clearance' refuses it. The tolerances it gives for information, H12, H13 and H14 for the three series, are not applied, nor is the chamfer it recommends where the hole's edge would meet the fillet under a head.

Counterbores

A counterbore sinks the head of a socket head cap screw below the surface. 'Counterbore', true takes IS 3406 (Part 2), type K, which it gives for the screws of IS 2269, the Indian adoption of ISO 4762, from M1.6 to M36: the diameter d2 and the depth t1. 'Counterbore', 'washer' takes its type K2, for the same screw on a plain washer of IS 2016, from M3 to M36: wider, d4, and deeper, t2. A plain washer of ISO 7089 fits it, 12 across and 1.6 thick for M6 against 13 and the 1.7 the counterbore deepens by.

The counterbore is the same over a fine and a medium clearance hole, which are the two series IS 3406 draws it with; over a coarse one it gives none, and a standard counterbore there is refused. Amendment 1 corrects two misprints of the table: the depth of type K at M5 is 5.7, printed 57, and the medium clearance hole at M12 is 13.5, printed 1.35. The table also prints a depth of type K at M1.4, below the M1.6 its scope begins at and below the smallest screw of ISO 4762, and the package leaves it out.

Countersinks

A countersink seats the head of a countersunk screw. IS 3406 (Part 1) gives one for each family of heads, all at 90 degrees:

  • Type A, 'Countersink', true, for the heads of ISO 7721: slotted, cross recessed and hexalobular countersunk screws, raised or not, as ISO 2009, 7046, 14581, 2010, 7047 and 14584, from M1 to M20. It names the Indian screws IS 1365, 7485, 7486 and 8911.
  • Type B, 'Countersink', 'socket', for hexagon socket countersunk screws, ISO 10642, from M3 to M24. It names the Indian screw IS 6761.

Type A has a series for each clearance hole, chosen by 'Clearance'. In the medium series it is a plain cone, d2 across at the surface. In the fine series it is a cone below a short cylinder of the same diameter, d3 across and t2 deep, which lets a head sit just below the surface. Type B is drawn in the fine series only. Neither gives a countersink over a coarse hole, and type B none over a medium one; those are refused.

The depth of the cone follows from the diameters at 90 degrees: half the difference between the countersink and the clearance hole, below the cylinder. The approximate depths t1 the tables print agree with it, and are not used. Type B steps up between M20 and M22, from 37 to 48.2 across, and on to 52 at M24. The depths printed for both are 0.5 less than their diameters give, where the smaller sizes agree exactly; as the two rows agree with each other, neither stands out as a misprint, and both are taken as printed.

Numbers instead

A counterbore or countersink of another size is given by its numbers, which are taken as given, with any clearance hole and with a plain diameter: [CD, CDEPTH] for a counterbore, and CD, [CD, ANGLE] or [CD, ANGLE, CDEPTH] for a countersink, the last a cylinder above the cone as in the fine series. A deeper counterbore, so that a screw of a standard length reaches, is the common case.

What is not done

  • The counterbores of IS 3406 (Part 2) for cheese and pan heads, its type H, and for screws on spring washers, its types K1 and K3.
  • The countersinks of IS 3406 (Part 1) for tapping screws, its type C at 80 degrees, and for countersunk bolts for steel structures, its type E.
  • The tolerances: H12 and H13 on the diameters, and the plus tolerances on the depths.
  • The 90 degree chamfer or radius IS 3406 (Part 2) asks for at the bottom edge of a counterbore from M12.
  • Counterbores for low head cap screws, DIN 7984, which IS 3406 does not give.

Editions

ISO 273:1979 is current, confirmed in 2024, and Europe adopts it as EN 20273. IS 3406 was prepared from DIN 74, parts 1 and 2, of 1980. Their successors are DIN 974-1, for counterbores, current in its edition of 2008, and ISO 15065:2005, for countersinks, confirmed in 2023. Neither was read, so where they differ from DIN 74 of 1980 the package follows the older values. The screws IS 3406 names are Indian Standards of the 1970s and 1980s, and the heads of the current ISO editions were not compared with them.

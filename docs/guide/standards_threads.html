Threads and tapped holes

The standards behind solid.Shape.hole with a thread name and solid.Shape.thread: where each number comes from, how it is computed, and what the package decides where a standard leaves the choice open.

The sources

Every number is read from a published standard or from a maker's catalogue, never from memory or from another library. The ISO standards were read in the Indian Standards that adopt them without change, which the Bureau of Indian Standards publishes free, so any value here can be checked without buying the standard.

Naming a thread

A thread is named as a drawing names it: 'M6' for a coarse thread, by its nominal diameter, and 'M10x1.25' for a fine one, with its pitch. The diameters and pitches are those of ISO 261, table 2, from M1 to M64. All three columns of the table are taken, and so are the pitches it prints in parentheses, which it asks to be avoided as far as possible: the choice is the designer's. A diameter with no coarse pitch, such as M15, is named with its pitch, 'M15x1.5'.

The basic profile

ISO 68-1 builds the thread on a triangle of height H = 0.866 025 P, its flanks at 60 degrees. From it:

  • D1 = d − 1.082 532 P, the minor diameter of the internal thread, twice 5H/8 below the nominal diameter d;
  • d2 = d − 0.649 519 P, the pitch diameter, twice 3H/8 below it;
  • the crest of the external thread is flat, P/8 wide, at the nominal diameter, and the crest of the internal thread flat, P/4 wide, at D1.

thread cuts this profile as it stands: an internal thread's root is flat at the nominal diameter, and an external thread's root flat at D1.

The 6H band

Every tapping drill lies in the 6H band of the minor diameter, from D1 up by the tolerance TD1 of grade 6, so a drawing that states 6H is met. ISO 965-1, 13.3.2, gives it in micrometres:

  • for a pitch from 0.2 to 0.8, TD1(6) = 433 P − 190 P1.22;
  • for a pitch of 1 and coarser, TD1(6) = 230 P0.7.

Its clause 13 says the tables hold these values rounded to the nearest number of the R 40 series of preferred numbers, then to a whole number, and that "in order to reproduce a smooth progression, these rules of rounding off have not always been used". The package rounds the same way. Where the R 40 number ends in a half, the table takes the even whole number, 26.5 becoming 26 and 31.5 becoming 32, and so does the package. That reproduces table 3 at every pitch ISO 261 uses but three, where the package takes the table's value: 140 micrometres at a pitch of 0.5, 236 at 1 and 450 at 2.5.

Table 3 has no grade 6 at the pitches 0.25 and 0.2, so there the band is the coarsest grade it has: grade 5 at 0.25, 56 micrometres from the table, and grade 4 at 0.2, 0.63 TD1(6) rounded, 38.

The tapping drill

Without options the drill is the one ISO 2306 lists, in table 1 for a coarse thread and in table 2 for a fine one: 5 for M6, 6.8 for M8, 8.8 for M10x1.25. For a thread it does not list, M60, M64 and many fine threads among them, the drill is the rule ISO 2306 states in its scope, the nominal diameter less the pitch: 58 for M64. Every drill ISO 2306 lists lies inside its band, and so does the nominal diameter less the pitch of every one of the 162 threads ISO 261 gives from M1 to M64.

Material and engagement

A tough material takes a larger drill, and so less thread, than a soft one. Tap makers give this as a percentage of full thread for each material, and OSG's chart asks 55 to 70 % in tough materials. In the makers' terms the 6H band of M6 lies between 65 and 83 %, so most of the holes the chart asks for would make a thread that is no longer 6H. The package keeps the band: 'Material' naming a tough material, 'stainless' or 'nickel', takes the largest drill inside the band, and every other material keeps the ISO 2306 drill. The tough materials are those OSG's chart names hard or tough: stainless steel, nickel steel, Monel, cast steel and drop forgings.

'Engagement' asks for the engagement itself, in ISO's terms rather than the makers': 100 % drills at D1, and the diameter asked is d − 1.0825 P times the percentage over 100. The makers count 100 % as 1.299 P off the diameter, so the same percentage is a different hole in the two. A percentage outside the band is refused, with the band it allows: 78.2 to 100 % for M6, 80.4 to 100 % for M8.

A drill chosen by 'Material' or 'Engagement' is the ISO 2306 drill or one of the sizes in RUKO's catalogue: every 0.1 millimetre from 0.3 to 13, the quarters from 1.25 to 9.75, every 0.5 to 50 and every millimetre to 60. Where the catalogue has no size inside a band, as for M1, the ISO 2306 drill stands.

External threads and the turned blank

A thread is external where its axis runs into a solid cylinder from halfway between the pitch diameter and the nominal diameter up to the nominal diameter: 5.675 to 6 for M6. A cylinder at the nominal diameter, which is how a rod is designed, is first turned down over the threaded length, as a machinist turns a blank, to the largest major diameter of tolerance class 6g: the nominal diameter plus the fundamental deviation of position g. ISO 965-1, 13.1, gives it as −(15 + 11 P) micrometres. Rounded as above, that reproduces its table 1 at every pitch but 0.75, where the table gives 22 and the rounding 24, and the package takes 22.

A cylinder below the nominal diameter is threaded as it is, its crest where the lathe left it, as an internal thread's crest stands where the drill left it.

How the thread is cut

The groove of the profile is swept round the axis by solid.helix, right-handed. It starts a pitch before the point, so that the thread runs out of the surface clean, and ends where its full profile reaches the depth; a thread right through runs a pitch past the far side of the part. Open CASCADE does not always cut the bare groove from a bored part, and can leave the part as it was without an error, so the groove is first joined to the cylinder of the bore, or to a sleeve outside the crest of a rod, and that tool is cut instead. The surfaces are splines fitted to the helix, so a thread's volume agrees with the area of its profile swept round the axis to about one part in ten thousand.

What is not done

  • Forming taps: the drawing states the thread, not how it is made, and a hole for a forming tap is drilled by its diameter.
  • Left-hand and multi-start threads, and inch threads.
  • Tolerances of the thread itself: the thread is cut at the basic profile. Only the tapping hole keeps to the 6H band, and only the turned blank to 6g.
  • A rounded root: the flat of the basic profile is cut.
  • Threading a hole twice: its bore is no longer a circle, and the second thread is refused.
  • The cosmetic thread of ISO 6410 on a drawing, which comes with the drawings of solids.

Editions

These are the editions read. ISO 965-1 has been revised since 1998: a fourth edition in 2013, amended in 2021, and a fifth in April 2026. The amendment of 2021 leaves the deviations, the tolerances and the rounding as they were. The texts of 2013 and 2026 were not read, and DIN's notes on its edition of 2026 list a revision of the calculation formulae, so the package follows the 1998 edition until the 2026 one has been read. Later editions of the other standards were not compared.
